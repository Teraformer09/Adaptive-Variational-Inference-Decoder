# Quantum Hardware Noise Physics and Motivation for Generative World Modeling

## 1. Spatio-Temporal and Schedule-Conditioned Transmon Noise

Superconducting quantum processors built from frequency-tunable transmons and tunable couplers exhibit error processes that are neither purely spatial nor purely temporal. Instead, physical decoherence on multi-qubit devices is simultaneously spatially correlated across coupler and substrate geometry, temporally non-stationary across repeated syndrome-extraction cycles, and directly conditioned on the active microwave and flux-pulse control schedule. Standard independent Pauli channel approximations fail to capture these coupled physical mechanisms because they assume memoryless, stationary, single-qubit or single-pair fault probabilities that remain invariant under changes in circuit concurrency.

The first major noise family comprises spatial and schedule-conditioned correlated errors (`A1` and `A2`). Under simultaneous two-qubit entangling gates (`A1`), residual static $ZZ$ interactions and higher-level transmon-coupler hybridization produce coherent phase accumulation and cross-resonances between actively driven pairs and spectator neighbors. Consequently, the effective circuit-level fault distribution changes whenever the set of concurrently executed gates changes. At a larger spatial scale (`A2`), cosmic-ray muons and background $\gamma$-ray impacts deposit energy into the silicon or sapphire substrate, generating non-equilibrium acoustic phonons that recombine Cooper pairs into quasiparticles. These bursts create radially decaying, chip-wide $T_1$ relaxation drops spanning cientos of micrometers and persisting for tens of microseconds across multiple syndrome rounds.

The second noise family arises from multi-level transmon leakage outside the computational qubit subspace (`B1`). Because the transmon is a weakly anharmonic oscillator ($\alpha / 2\pi \approx -200\text{ to }-300\text{ MHz}$), off-resonant microwave drives, diabatic flux ramps during conditional-phase ($\text{CZ}$) gates, and thermal heating excite population into the second excited state $|2\rangle$ and higher levels. Once a data or ancilla transmon leaks into $|2\rangle$, it persists there for many consecutive rounds until seepage or active reset returns it to $\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\}$. During its leaked lifetime, dispersive shifts scramble subsequent stabilizer parity measurements, yielding anomalous detection-event streams with approximately $50\%\text{–}60\%$ defect rates that cannot be represented within a two-level Pauli channel.

The third noise family encompasses non-stationary parameter drift and non-Markovian environmental memory (`C1` and `C2`). Superconducting Josephson junctions and dielectric interfaces host microscopic two-level-system (TLS) defects whose transition frequencies fluctuate due to charge and strain reconfiguration (`C1`). When a transmon is driven by continuous or repeated microwave pulses, coherent energy exchange with resonant or near-resonant TLS defects induces drive-dependent dephasing and memory revivals. Even during idle intervals (`C2`), coupling to a structured environment of coherent TLS modes produces non-Markovian population and phase revivals characterized by temporarily negative canonical time-convolutionless (TCL) decoherence rates $\gamma_{\mathrm{TCL}}(t) < 0$, violating the complete-positivity semigroup assumptions of standard time-homogeneous Lindblad master equations.

## 2. The Exact Simulation Wall and the Role of the World Model

Exact open-system master-equation solvers such as QuTiP and QuTiP-QIP provide faithful physical reference dynamics for small subsystems of $N \le 4$ qutrits or composite qubit–TLS registers. However, representing a density matrix $\rho$ over $N$ $d$-level systems requires $d^{2N}$ complex numbers, and constructing the Liouvillian superoperator $\mathcal{L}$ scales as $O(d^{4N})$. For a modest distance-3 Surface-17 processor ($N = 17$ qutrits, $d = 3$), a single density matrix requires $3^{34} \approx 1.66 \times 10^{16}$ complex amplitudes ($\approx 266\text{ PB}$), rendering multi-round exact open-system simulation computationally intractable. Conversely, scalable stabilizer simulators such as Stim operate in $O(N^2)$ time per round but are restricted to Markovian Clifford circuits and stochastic Pauli channels.

The AVID World Model bridges this gap by acting as a **learned spatio-temporal noise generator and predictive latent noise-state estimator**. Calibrated against exact small-system QuTiP physical references and classical Bayesian baselines, the world model consumes only experimentally accessible classical observations—syndrome detection events, raw stabilizer outcomes, device topology, active gate schedules, and validity-masked calibration/analog telemetry—to infer a structured posterior belief over the hidden physical noise regimes and forecast future fault statistics across multiple QEC rounds.
