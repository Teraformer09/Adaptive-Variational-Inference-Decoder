# Benchmark Suite and Classical Baseline Evaluation Protocol

## 1. Five Physical Noise Benchmarks and Mixed-Noise Disentanglement

To evaluate quantum hardware noise generation and latent noise-state estimation without conflating performance with downstream QEC decoding, the benchmark suite defines five isolated physical noise benchmarks plus a unified sixth mixed-noise benchmark (`MIXED_ALL_THREE`). Each benchmark is anchored to a peer-reviewed superconducting hardware study, with exact open-system parameters recorded in `Benchmark-Evals/Qutip-Baseline/configs/benchmark_configs.json` and validated against QuTiP 5.2.3 master-equation simulations.

The first benchmark (`B1_TRANSMON_LEAKAGE`) models three-level $\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle, |2\rangle\}$ transmon leakage and seepage during repeated Surface-17 conditional-phase ($\text{CZ}$) stabilizer cycles following Varbanov et al. (2020). QuTiP qutrit simulations verify that a data transmon leaked into $|2\rangle$ induces a dispersive phase shift on coupled ancillas that scrambles stabilizer parity checks with a $58.26\%$ defect probability per round. Estimators are evaluated on per-transmon leakage precision–recall area under the curve (PR-AUC), F1 score, and detection delay in QEC rounds under both syndrome-only and analog readout log-likelihood ratio (`LLR`) conditioning.

The second benchmark (`A1_GATE_CROSSTALK`) models schedule-conditioned coherent $ZZ$ and cross-resonance crosstalk during simultaneous entangling gates following Ketterer & Wellens (2023). QuTiP Pauli-twirled process tomography demonstrates that executing parallel two-qubit gates increases joint pairwise fault probabilities by more than an order of magnitude ($13.13\times$ correlation ratio) relative to isolated single-gate schedules. Estimators are evaluated on pairwise correlated fault root-mean-square error (RMSE) across both in-distribution gate schedules and unseen out-of-distribution (`OOD`) three-gate concurrency schedules.

The third and fourth benchmarks (`C1_DRIVEN_TLS` and `C2_IDLE_MEMORY`) capture non-stationary parameter drift and non-Markovian environmental memory following Agarwal et al. (2023) and Gulácsi & Burkard (2023). In `C1`, strong Rabi drives ($10\text{–}50\text{ MHz}$) couple a transmon to a resonant two-level-system (TLS) defect, producing drive-conditioned dephasing growth on mirrored pseudoidentity sequences $U(\theta) = X_{-\theta}X_\theta$. In `C2`, coupling to coherent TLS modes during idle intervals yields non-Markovian coherence revivals with negative canonical time-convolutionless decay rates ($\min_t \gamma_{\mathrm{TCL}}(t) = -20.17\ \mu\mathrm{s}^{-1}$). Estimators are evaluated on multi-horizon latent rate RMSE and predictive negative log-likelihood (NLL) across forecast horizons $H \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$.

The fifth benchmark (`A2_CHIPWIDE_EVENTS`) models cosmic-ray and background radiation phonon bursts across a millimeter-scale transmon array following Wilen et al. (2021), characterized by radial exponential footprint decay ($\lambda_{\mathrm{phonon}} = 400\ \mu\mathrm{m}$) and $\tau_r \approx 15\ \mu\mathrm{s}$ relaxation recovery. Finally, the sixth benchmark (`MIXED_ALL_THREE`) injects simultaneous schedule crosstalk, transmon leakage, slow calibration drift, TLS memory revivals, and chip-wide phonon bursts onto the 17-node Surface-17 processor graph to test whether an estimator can disentangle coexisting physical fault causes without cross-channel false positives.

## 2. Classical Bayesian Baselines (`B0–B5`) and Split Integrity

Every neural world model variant is benchmarked head-to-head against five non-neural classical noise estimators plus an exact simulator oracle upper bound on identical held-out trajectories. `B0_STATIC` assumes stationary, independent per-detector Bernoulli error rates calibrated from training averages. `B1_STATIC_CORR` estimates a static pairwise correlation matrix from historical syndrome co-occurrences without conditioning on the gate schedule $u_t$. `B2_HMM` applies a two-state forward Hidden Markov Model per transmon for leakage and burst state tracking. `B3_EMPIRICAL_TRACKER` combines schedule-lookup tables, spatial exponential kernels, and exponential moving-average variational updates. `B4_AR1_OU_FILTER` applies a continuous-state Ornstein–Uhlenbeck / Kalman filter to track slow drift, while `B5_ORACLE_REF` accesses the exact simulator `latent_truth` to establish the theoretical Bayes-optimal performance ceiling.

To prevent temporal data leakage, all trajectory records are validated against `world_model/dataset/trajectory.schema.json` and partitioned into `train`, `val`, `test_id`, and `test_ood_schedule` strictly by complete trajectories and disjoint random seeds—never by slicing individual rounds from the same trajectory across splits.
